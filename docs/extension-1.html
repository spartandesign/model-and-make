A Robot Bracket on the V5 Pattern

Optional, for early finishers. Robots are built from metal with holes every 0.5 in. In this extension you design a printed bracket that bolts onto real VEX V5 metal, using the same clearance skills you used for your fingerboard holes.

🛠️ PLTW ProjectExtension 1 · when you finish (ask your teacher)

A PLTW Project asks you to use skills you already have to make something real, with clear requirements and limits.

🎯 Your goal

  • I can place holes on the VEX 0.5 in pattern.
  • I can pick a screw clearance hole by testing it, like the Week 9 bolt holes.
  • I can explain why one screw acts like a hinge.

🧰 You’ll need

  • Onshape (inches)
  • Calipers
  • A #8-32 screw and a piece of V5 metal (ask your teacher)
  • The bracket designer on this page

⏳ Time and limits

  • Self-paced: about 2 sessions of 45 minutes, plus print time.
  • Finish your regular chapter work first. This is extra.
  • Your teacher prints it when there’s printer time.
  • Prints flat, with no supports.

Step-by-step plan

Follow the steps in order. The minutes are a guide. Check off each checkpoint before you move on.

Session 1 · about 45 min

  1. 8 minWatch the video.
  2. 10 minCaliper a real V5 part: the hole spacing and the screw.
  3. 12 minPlan a bracket on the bracket designer. Copy the hole centers.
  4. 15 minModel a hole-test coupon: 0.170, 0.180, and 0.190 in holes. Submit it.

✔ Checkpoint: The coupon is submitted, and your bracket plan is done.

Session 2 · about 45 min

  1. 10 minTest the coupon with the real screw. Pick the hole that fits.
  2. 25 minModel the bracket with that hole size, on the 0.5 in pattern.
  3. 10 minCheck that it prints flat, then submit. Take the self-check.

✔ Checkpoint: Your bracket uses the tested hole size and is submitted.

The 0.5 inch pattern

VEX V5 metal has square holes every 0.5 in. Count holes and multiply by 0.5: a part 6 holes long is about 3 in. Check it with your calipers, the same way you did in Week 1.

One screw is a hinge

A bracket held by one screw can spin around it. Use at least two screws, and put one out of line if the bracket gets pushed sideways.

Same skill as your fingerboard holes

V5 uses #8-32 screws, about 0.164 in across. Like your Week 9 truck holes, a printed hole must be bigger than the screw, so test it. Print a small coupon with three holes and keep the one that fits without forcing.

Goal: a tested hole size

Print a test coupon

Model a small plate with holes at 0.170, 0.180, and 0.190 in. Submit it.

✅ Check it: your teacher has the coupon file.

Goal: holes on the pattern

Sketch the bracket

Use the hole centers from the bracket designer. Every hole is a multiple of 0.5 in from one corner.

✅ Check it: measure two holes in Onshape. Their distance is a multiple of 0.5 in.

Goal: a part that bolts on

Extrude, cut, and test

Extrude the plate, cut the holes at your tested size, and submit. Then bolt it to the metal.

✅ Check it: two screws go in without forcing, and the bracket can’t spin.
