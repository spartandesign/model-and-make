Watch first: parts, revolve, and mates

Read the transcript

For two weeks, you'll model a skateboard in Onshape. The skills you learn here build your fingerboard and your skate park later. A skateboard is a set of parts that fit together. The deck. The trucks: a baseplate, a hanger, and a bushing. And the wheels, each with a bearing. To make round parts, you use revolve. Draw a flat profile next to an axis, then spin it all the way around. That's how you make wheels and bearings. Later, it's how you could make a ramp or a bowl. In the Assembly, mates connect the parts. A wheel mate leaves one degree of freedom: it can spin, but it can't fall off. The deck is fixed, so it doesn't move at all. Pay close attention to the truck bolt holes. Their spacing decides whether the trucks fit. This week the board is full size. In Week 9, you'll measure real fingerboard trucks and place holes that actually fit.

Week 6 — model the deck and trucks

  • 2.1 Designing the Deck. Fully constrain a deck sketch, extrude, fillet, and add the truck bolt-hole pattern.
  • 2.2 Designing the Trucks. Build the three truck parts (baseplate, hanger, bushing) with materials and mass.

Week 7 — wheels, bearings, and assembly

  • 2.3 Wheels and Bearings. Use revolve to make round parts, with construction lines and diameter dimensions.
  • 2.4 Putting It All Together. In the Assembly tab, fix the deck, make the wheels spin, insert hardware, and use mate connectors and degrees of freedom.

Lesson 2.5 “Now Shred!” is held for Week 10 — after your deck is printed, you’ll come back and customize it.

Try it: take the board apart

A skateboard is a set of separate parts. Pull it apart to see each one, then spin the wheels: a wheel mate leaves exactly one degree of freedom.

One thing you add

  • Bolt-hole connection note (after 2.1). In your paper notebook, sketch the truck-mounting layout and write one sentence on why hole placement matters for fit. Those holes are the exact Week 9 problem: you’ll caliper the real fingerboard trucks and place mounting holes that actually fit.

Looking ahead: the mates and degrees of freedom from 2.4 are the same skill the 12 capstone groups use to snap park sections to one connection standard; and the revolve from 2.3 is how you’d make a ramp or bowl. Early finishers can start a “Skate Ramp” Part Studio — a real head start on capstone design.

Heads-up on scale: Unit 2 models a full-size skateboard. Your Week 9 fingerboard deck is modeled at real fingerboard scale (96 mm), measured with calipers — not a shrunk-down copy of this board. You’ll re-apply the same skills at actual fingerboard dimensions.
