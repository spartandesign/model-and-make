3D Printers: Bambu P2S & A1 mini

A 3D printer is a hot glue gun on a robot. It melts a plastic string (filament), squeezes it through a tiny nozzle, and draws your part one thin layer at a time. Our P2S printers and the A1 mini work the same way and use the same software.

How it works

  1. Slice. Bambu Studio cuts your 3D model into hundreds of thin layers (about 0.2 mm each) and writes the path for the nozzle on every layer. That file is G-code.
  2. Heat. The nozzle heats to melt the filament, and the bed warms so the first layer sticks.
  3. Calibrate. The printer checks the bed height at many points, so the first layer is the same thickness everywhere. You’ll hear buzzing and clicking. That’s normal.
  4. Draw layers. The extruder gears push filament into the hot end. Melted plastic comes out of the nozzle, the fan cools it, and the bed steps down (P2S) or the head steps up (A1 mini) for the next layer.
  5. Cool and remove. When the plate is cool, lift it off and flex it. The print pops off.

Remember Chapter 7: printers can’t print in mid-air. Overhangs past about 45° need supports, and the flat face of your part goes down.

How it’s set up in our lab

  • Each printer sits on a flat, steady table, away from drafts, with space behind it for air.
  • Printers are on the school Wi-Fi and bound to the class Bambu account, so Bambu Studio can send files to them. Your teacher manages that account.
  • The textured PEI plates stay in the printers. Spare plates and the scraper live by the printers.
  • Filament is stored dry in sealed bins with desiccant (see Filament).

Before every print

Goal: a clean, empty printer

Check the bed

The plate is clean, with no old print or plastic bits, and it’s seated flat on the pins at the back.

✅ Check it: run a finger (on a cool plate) across it: no bumps, no grease.

Goal: the right plastic

Check the filament

The screen (or AMS) shows the filament you sliced for, and there’s enough on the spool. Bambu Studio shows how many grams you need.

✅ Check it: filament type and color on the printer match your slice.

Goal: a clear path

Check the space

Nothing on top of or behind the printer. For the A1 mini, nothing in the bed’s path in front or behind.

✅ Check it: the bed and head can move freely.

Bambu Studio: from model to print

Goal: your part on the plate

Import

In Onshape, export your part as STL or 3MF. In Bambu Studio: File → Import, or drag the file onto the plate.

✅ Check it: your part shows on the plate.

Goal: the right printer and plastic

Pick printer, plate, and filament

On the left, choose the printer (P2S or A1 mini), the plate (Textured PEI), and the filament (for example Bambu PLA Basic).

✅ Check it: the printer, plate, and filament names at the top left match the real printer.

Goal: a part that sits right

Place it

Use Lay on face (F) to put the biggest flat face down, and Arrange (A) to center it. Keep it inside the plate lines.

✅ Check it: no red warning, and the flat face is on the plate.

Goal: supports only where needed

Choose supports

Leave supports off unless your part has overhangs. If it does, turn on Enable support and pick tree (auto).

✅ Check it: the preview shows supports under overhangs, and nowhere else.

Goal: a plan you checked

Slice and preview

Click Slice plate. Drag the layer slider on the right to check the first layer and the overhangs. Read the time and grams.

✅ Check it: the first layer touches the plate everywhere, and the time fits your class schedule.

Goal: a print that starts

Send it

Click Print plate, choose your printer, and leave Bed leveling on. Then go to the printer and watch the first layer.

✅ Check it: you watched the first layer go down smooth and flat.

Safety

It looks or sounds scary…What’s really going on
Loud buzzing and clicking before the print startsCalibration. The printer is measuring the bed and tuning its motors. The A1 mini’s motor noise test is especially loud. All normal.
It spits out a little blob of plasticPurging old plastic before printing. The P2S drops it out the back through a chute.
A burnt smellNot normal. Stop the print and tell your teacher.
The bed moves toward you (A1 mini)That’s how it prints. Keep hands and sleeves clear while it runs.

⚠️ What can actually hurt you

  • The nozzle reaches up to 300 °C. Never touch it, even right after a print.
  • The bed reaches 110 °C on the P2S. Let it cool before you lift the plate.
  • Moving parts can pinch. Hands out until the print is done and the head is parked.
  • Use the scraper away from your body, and only on a plate that’s out of the printer.
