Sketching & Dimensioning

Put a design on paper so anyone could build it · work solo or with a partner. A model in your head or on a screen only helps you. A drawing communicates a design to everyone else — a teammate, a machinist, or your future self — with enough information to build and measure the part. This week you learn to draw an object in standard views and dimension it by hand. It’s a pencil-and-paper skill now; in Week 11 it becomes a real CAD drawing of your fingerboard deck.

The three standard views

Engineers describe a 3D object with three flat views, laid out in a fixed arrangement (third-angle projection):

  • Front — the most descriptive face of the object.
  • Top — placed directly above the front view.
  • Right side — placed directly to the right of the front view.

Lined up this way, the views share edges, so a measurement on one lines up with the others.

The isometric view

Add one isometric view — the object drawn at an angle so it looks 3D — so a reader can see the whole shape at a glance. The three flat views give the exact sizes; the isometric shows how they go together.

Dimensioning rules

  • Use extension and dimension lines. Thin lines that show exactly which feature a number describes.
  • Dimension each feature once. Don’t repeat a size on multiple views — pick the clearest place and put it there.
  • Show overall sizes and feature sizes. The object’s overall length, width, and height, plus every hole, slot, or step.
  • State the units. Note whether the drawing is in inches or millimeters.
  • Keep it readable. Numbers off the part, lines uncluttered, nothing left to guess.

Do it — draw a real object

  • Pick a simple object. Something with a couple of features — a wooden block, an eraser, a small box, a LEGO brick.
  • Measure it. Use your Week 1 caliper skills to measure the overall size and every feature.
  • Draw the three views. Sketch the front, top, and right views in the third-angle layout.
  • Add the isometric view. Sketch the object at an angle so its 3D shape is clear.
  • Dimension everything. Add overall length, width, and height, plus each feature — each dimensioned once, with units noted.
  • Title the sheet. Put the object name, your name, the date, and the units in a corner.

What to submit

  • Your sketched drawing: three standard views plus an isometric view, fully dimensioned.
  • One sentence — the trickiest feature to dimension, and how you decided where to put it.
