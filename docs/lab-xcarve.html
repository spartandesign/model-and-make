X-Carve Pro CNC Router

A CNC router is a robot drill that moves sideways. A spinning bit cuts into wood or plastic while the machine moves it along a path from Easel. It’s the strongest machine in the lab, and also one of the simplest once you know its parts.

Name the parts

How it works

  1. Design in Easel (or import an SVG from Onshape or TinkerCAD). Tell Easel your material, its size and thickness, and your bit.
  2. Easel makes a toolpath: the route the bit follows, and how deep it goes on each pass.
  3. The machine homes (finds its corners), then you set zero: where the corner and the top of your material are.
  4. The spindle spins the bit, and the motors move it in X, Y, and Z. The bit cuts a little deeper each pass until it reaches the depth you set.

Lasers burn, but a CNC cuts, so it can carve thick wood, pockets, and 3D shapes. Unlike the lasers, it can also cut some plastics that are banned in the laser, like HDPE, because nothing burns.

Easel’s bit library sets a safe feed rate (how fast it moves) and depth per pass for each bit and material. Don’t change them without your teacher.

Try it: zero, depth, and tabs

The two most important numbers in CNC are where zero is and how deep you cut. Try different settings and watch what the bit does.

How it’s set up in our lab

  • The X-Carve Pro plugs into its own regular outlet, not a GFCI outlet (Inventables says it isn’t compatible with them).
  • A lab laptop running Easel connects to the controller by USB.
  • The shop vacuum connects to the dust shoe hose. It runs during every carve.
  • Safety glasses and hearing protection hang next to the machine. The E-stop on the control box is always reachable.

Easel: your first carve

Goal: a design Easel understands

Set up the job

In Easel, choose X-Carve Pro. Enter the material and its real size and thickness (measure with calipers). Choose your bit.

✅ Check it: the material thickness in Easel matches your caliper reading.

Goal: a design that fits

Design and set depth

Import your SVG or draw shapes. For each shape, choose the cut path (outside, inside, on the line, or fill) and the depth. Add tabs to parts that cut all the way through.

✅ Check it: through-cuts are set to the material thickness, with tabs.

Goal: a path you checked

Preview

Look at the 3D preview and the estimated time. Every clamp must be outside the cut area.

✅ Check it: the preview shows your part, and no path crosses a clamp.

Goal: material that can’t move

Clamp it

Clamp the material flat to the wasteboard with at least two clamps, placed where the bit and dust shoe can’t hit them. Jog the dust shoe around the clamps to check.

✅ Check it: push on the material: it doesn’t move.

Goal: the machine knows where things are

Home and zero

Click Carve and follow the steps: home the machine, put in the bit, use the Z-probe on top of your material, then jog to the corner to set X and Y zero.

✅ Check it: Easel says zero is set, and the bit is at the front-left corner of your material.

Goal: a carve you watched

Carve

Glasses and hearing protection on. Turn on the vacuum. Start the carve. Stay with it, hand near the controls. When it’s done, wait for the spindle to stop before reaching in.

✅ Check it: the spindle stopped before your hands went in.

Safety

It looks or sounds scary…What’s really going on
It’s really loudA spindle at high speed plus a vacuum is loud. That’s why you wear hearing protection. The sound should stay steady.
Chips flyingThe dust shoe catches most. Glasses protect your eyes from the rest.
The gantry moves fast when it homesHoming is quick but always the same path. Keep hands off the machine bed while it moves.

⚠️ What can actually hurt you

  • A spinning bit. Hands out until the spindle has stopped completely.
  • Loose things get grabbed: tie back long hair, no hanging sleeves, hoodie strings, lanyards, or gloves.
  • Flying chips and noise: safety glasses and hearing protection, every time.
  • Fire is rare but possible from friction and dust. Never leave a carve running.
  • The E-stop is your best friend. Know where it is before you start.
