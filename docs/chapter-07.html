Design for Printing

How to design so it actually prints · work solo or in pairs. You can model almost anything in Onshape now — but a part that looks perfect on screen can still fail on the printer: weak in the wrong direction, full of messy supports, or with features too small to form. This week you learn the rules that make a part print well, then use them to plan the fingerboard deck you print next week. The whole class shares the printers, so a deck designed wrong wastes a print and costs someone a turn in the queue — getting the design right here is what keeps Week 9 on track.

The five rules of design for printing

  • Orientation = strength. A print is built in layers from the bed up, and it’s weakest between layers. Place the part so the layers run along the direction it will be stressed — print the deck flat so the layers run along it, not across the kicktail.
  • Avoid supports. The printer can’t lay plastic on thin air; any surface overhanging more than about 45° from vertical needs support material that wastes plastic and leaves a rough surface. Keep big flat bottoms on the bed and use angled chamfers instead of sharp overhangs — aim to print with no supports at all.
  • Mind the minimum feature size. The nozzle is about 0.4 mm wide, so very thin walls, pegs, or raised text won’t form. Keep any feature at least ~1 mm thick. (Printers think in millimeters; we design in inches — convert when you need to: 1 in = 25.4 mm.)
  • First layer and bed contact. The first layer squishes onto the bed, so a big flat bottom sticks well (and may read a touch wide — the “elephant’s foot” from your Week 5 cube). A tiny contact area can pop loose mid-print; add a brim to hold it down.
  • Clearance for fit. Two parts that must fit can’t be modeled the same size. Make the hole bigger than the peg by a clearance, and size that clearance from your measured Week 5 deviation plus a little room to slide. Too tight = interference (won’t go on); too loose = slop.

Clearance is the rule that carries the rest of the course: it’s how your Week 9 deck holes fit the calipered trucks, and how the capstone sections snap to one connection standard. Also remember the build area: each capstone section must fit ≤ 180 mm (the A1 Mini bed, so it can print on any of our printers) — if a part is bigger, split it into sections that connect.
