Unit 3 · The Fingerboard · Week 9

Print the Deck + Fit the Trucks

Your fingerboard becomes real · work solo. This is where everything comes together. You’ll model a real-scale fingerboard deck, print it, and fit it to trucks you measured yourself — the first time you make a printed part fit a real, purchased part. Every skill so far shows up at once: calipers (Wk 1), the print-and-measure loop (Wk 5), the deck’s mounting holes (Unit 2), and the clearance rule (Wk 8).

Units note — this part is in millimeters. Fingerboards are a metric standard (96 mm long, 32 mm wide), so switch your digital caliper to mm and model the deck in mm. That’s the Week 8 conversion in action (1 in = 25.4 mm). Your Week 5 deviation of about 0.01 in is roughly 0.25 mm — useful for picking clearance.

Monday — measure your trucks

Before you can design the holes, you need the real numbers. Get your trucks and your digital caliper (set to mm).

  • Measure the bolt diameter. Caliper the mounting bolt/post that goes through the deck, and record it.
  • Measure the hole spacing. Caliper the distance between mounting holes on the truck baseplate — both across and along. Sketch the pattern in your notebook with the numbers.
  • Don’t guess. Every brand is a little different — your numbers come from your trucks, not from a chart.

Block 1 — model the deck and submit

  • Model the deck. New Onshape document in mm. Make the deck 96 mm long × 32 mm wide and add the nose/tail shape you want.
  • Place the mounting holes. Use the spacing you measured to position the holes. Size each hole = bolt diameter + clearance (start from ~0.25–0.4 mm, based on your Week 5 deviation).
  • Check orientation. Confirm the deck will print flat (Week 8 rule) so the kicktail is strong and no supports are needed.
  • Submit. Export the print file with your name and submit it.

Between sessions your teacher prints all the decks flat — they’ll be waiting next class. You designed the clearance carefully so it fits the first time; the whole class shares the printers, so a reprint costs someone a turn in the queue.

Block 2 — fit, mount, and log

  • Test the fit. Get your printed deck. Do the truck bolts go through the holes — too tight, just right, or too loose?
  • Mount the hardware. Attach the purchased trucks and wheels. You now have a working fingerboard.
  • Complete the fit log. Record what you measured, what you designed, and how the fit turned out.
  • Plan one change. If it was tight or loose, what clearance would you use next time?

What you’re really testing: tolerance

The fit you just felt is tolerance made real. A hole sized exactly to the bolt won’t go on — that’s interference. A hole far too big lets the trucks wobble — that’s slop. The clearance you chose is the in-between, and now you know whether your number was right for this printer. Hold onto that number: the capstone uses the very same idea, when 12 groups design park sections that snap to one shared connection standard.

What to submit

  • A photo of your assembled, working fingerboard.
  • Your completed fit log.
  • A photo or sketch of the truck mounting pattern you measured, with numbers.
  • One sentence — was your clearance right, and what would you change?
