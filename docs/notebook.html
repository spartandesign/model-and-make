Getting Started

Your Engineering Notebook

Engineers keep a record of how a design came to be. In this class that record is a Google Slides engineering notebook, the same kind the Competitive Robotics teams keep. Short written work goes in Google Docs.

Make your copy (Week 1)

  1. Sign in with your school Google account.
  2. Open the notebook template and click Make a copy.
  3. Name it P1 Last First — Engineering Notebook.
  4. Click Share and add your teacher as an editor (or set it to “Anyone at Downey USD with the link can comment”).
  5. Bookmark it. You keep this one copy all semester.

Four slides per project

Every project in Chapters 1–13 has a section with four slides. The colors match the rubric.

SlideWhat goes on it
DefineThe goal, the requirements, and the limits, in your own words. A sketch of the problem.
DevelopSketches, CAD screenshots, or drawings with key dimensions. The choices you made, and why.
OptimizeThe made part, with calipers or a ruler in the photo. Designed vs. measured. Test results and fixes.
ReflectWhat worked, what you’d change, and what you learned. Use your numbers.

✅ Habits that are graded

  • Sign and date every slide.
  • Credit sources right where you use them.
  • Numbers beat “it works.”
  • Your words, your work.

In Week 15, one teammate opens the team notebook template, makes a copy named P1 Team## — Capstone Notebook, and shares it with the team and the teacher as editors. Everyone writes in that one copy. Every slide gets the author’s initials and the date.

How it’s graded

Each project is graded on its own assignment. On top of that, three Notebook Checkpoints score the whole notebook, using five categories adapted from the robotics teams’ notebook rubric:

CategoryPointsLooks like
Organization8Contents up to date, colors used, every slide signed and dated, sources credited
Define6Goal, requirements, and limits for each project
Develop Solutions8Sketches, CAD, drawings with key dimensions, and reasons
Optimize6Made-part photos, designed vs. measured, tests, fixes
Reflection & Iteration4Specific changes and why, with numbers

Each category has four levels: Exemplary, Proficient, Developing, Beginning. Checkpoints are due Thursday of Week 5 (§1–5), Week 14 (§6–13), and Week 20 (team notebook).
