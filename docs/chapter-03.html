Watch first: design a stand that fits

Read the transcript

This week you design your first part that has to fit a real object: a stand for a fingerboard. Start with the real board. Caliper the deck length, the deck width, the height with the wheels on, and how far the wheels stick out. Write down every number. Your stand is an easel: a back the board leans on, and a ledge it rests on. Make the back a little shorter than the deck, so the board still shows. Make the ledge deep enough that the wheels clear, and the board drops in without forcing. Your teacher cuts every stand on the laser, from one shared Onshape file. Solid lines cut all the way through. Dotted lines are scored, so they fold cleanly. Fold on the scored lines, glue the tabs, and set your board on it. Does it sit at a nice angle, with the wheels clear and nothing forced? Write down one thing you'd change.

What you do

  • Measure the demo board. With your caliper, measure the assembled demo fingerboard your teacher prints: deck length, deck width, the overall height with the trucks and wheels on, and how far the wheels stick out past the deck. Record every value.
  • Draw and dimension your stand. Sketch an easel-style stand — a folded back the board leans against and a front ledge it rests on. Dimension it from your measurements: make the back a little shorter than the deck so the board shows, the width match the deck, and the ledge deep enough that the wheels clear and the board drops in without forcing. This dimensioned drawing is your deliverable.
  • See the CAD preview. Your teacher will show the same stand drawn in Onshape — a preview of the CAD you start next week. Same part, two ways to describe it: your hand drawing and the model.
  • Get your cut stand. Your teacher cuts the stands on the laser from one shared Onshape file. Solid lines cut all the way through; dotted fold lines are scored so they bend cleanly. You design and measure; your teacher runs the laser.
  • Fold and assemble. Fold on the scored lines, glue the tabs, and stand it up.
  • Check the fit. Set your board on the stand. Does it sit at a nice angle, with the wheels clearing and nothing forced? Note one thing you’d change.

Try it: will the board drop in?

The wheels and trucks stick out below the deck. Slide the ledge depth and watch what happens. For this stand, a little roomy is fine. Precise fits come in Weeks 8–9.

What to submit

  • Your dimensioned drawing of the stand, with the board measurements it’s based on.
  • A photo of your assembled stand with the demo board displayed in it.
  • One sentence: one thing you’d change to make it fit or display better.

Reminders

  • You design and measure; your teacher runs the laser.
  • Everyone’s stand is cut from the same Onshape master, so the parts are identical.
  • Bring a cereal box — cut on the printed side and your stand carries that design; you can engrave a name too.
  • Make the ledge and back a little roomy — a loose fit now is fine; precise fit comes in Weeks 8–9.
  • The laser burns away a thin strip as it cuts (the kerf); you’ll measure yours for real in Week 12.
