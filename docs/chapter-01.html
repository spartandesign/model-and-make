Measurement & Calipers

Measure precisely — the skill the whole course is built on · work solo or with a partner. Everything you build this semester starts with measuring it accurately. This week you learn to use calipers to measure to a thousandth of an inch (0.001 in) — far finer than a ruler — and to record what you measure so someone else could rebuild the part. Calipers don’t go back in the drawer after this week; you’ll use them in every unit.

Watch first: measure like an engineer

Read the transcript

Everything you build in this class starts with a measurement. A ruler gets you close. A caliper gets you to one thousandth of an inch. A caliper measures three ways. The outside jaws grip thickness and diameter. The inside jaws reach into openings. The depth rod slides out of the end to measure how deep a hole or step is. And the readout shows the number. Before you measure, close the jaws all the way. If the screen doesn't read zero, press the zero button. Now it reads zero point zero zero zero. Open the jaws and set the part between them. Sit the jaws square, across the flat faces, and close them gently. Don't crank them. Read the number, and write it down with its units. A dial caliper is read in two steps. First, the bar. The slider is past one point two inches. Then, the dial. The needle points to thirty-seven. That means thirty-seven thousandths. Add them. One point two, plus zero point zero three seven, is one point two three seven inches. Need millimeters? Press the units button. Zero point five one two inches becomes thirteen point zero zero millimeters, because one inch is twenty-five point four millimeters. Then, check your work. Measure again, or trade with a partner. If your readings don't match, measure again until you agree on the true value. Remember. Zero first. Use the right jaws. Square, gentle, and across flat faces. Bar plus dial on a dial caliper. And always write the units.

Meet your calipers

  • Dial caliper. Outside jaws for thickness and diameter, inside jaws for openings, and a depth rod. Read the whole number off the bar, then the fine number off the dial — together they give you 0.001 in.
  • Digital caliper. Same jaws, but the measurement shows on a screen. Press ZERO with the jaws fully closed so it reads 0.000, and use the in/mm button to switch units.
  • Good technique (both). Close the jaws gently — don’t crank them — sit them square on the feature, and measure across flat faces, not rounded edges or corners.

Try it: read a dial caliper

The dial caliper is the tricky one, so practice here first. Drag the slider to move the jaw and watch the bar and dial change. Then press New challenge and read it yourself.

The rule: bar first, then dial. The bar gives inches and tenths. The dial gives thousandths. Add them together.

Measure and log

  • Gather objects. Get the set of objects from your teacher (or pick several small classroom objects).
  • Measure to 0.001 in. For each object, measure the feature listed in the log — a thickness, a diameter, a depth, or an opening — using the right part of the caliper.
  • Check it. Re-measure each object, or trade with a partner and compare. If your readings differ, measure again and agree on the true value — that’s how real metrology works.
  • Try the toggle. On the digital caliper, switch one measurement to millimeters and record it too — you’ll work in mm when you measure fingerboard parts in Week 9.
