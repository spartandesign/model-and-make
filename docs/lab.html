How a license works

A license means you’ve shown you can run one kind of machine safely. You earn it in three steps:

  1. Learn it. Read the machine’s page, watch the videos, and practice naming its parts with the diagram.
  2. Pass the quiz. Take the license quiz on Canvas. You need 100% on the safety questions, and you can retake it.
  3. Show it. Do every step on the practical sign-off checklist on the real machine, without help, while your teacher watches.

🔁 Licenses can be paused

A license can be paused if a safety rule is broken. Usually that means a quick re-check with your teacher, not starting over. Your teacher can also add a rule for a machine at any time.

Five rules for every machine

  • Only run a machine you hold a license for, and only when your teacher is in the room and says go.
  • Stay with the machine the whole time it runs. A laser job is never left alone, not even for a minute.
  • If something looks, smells, or sounds wrong, stop the machine (Pause, Stop, or the E-stop) and get your teacher. You will never be in trouble for stopping a job.
  • Hands out of a machine while it moves. Wait for it to finish, cool, and stop.
  • Leave the machine clean and ready for the next person, and write any problem on the lab log.

Scared of the machines? Good instinct. Here’s the real picture.

Respect is right. Fear usually comes from not knowing what a machine is doing. These are the things that can actually hurt you:

MachineWhat can actually hurt youWhat protects you
3D printersA nozzle up to 300 °C and a bed up to 110 °C. Moving parts (the A1 mini’s bed swings forward and back).Wait until it stops and cools. Use the scraper or flex the plate, never your fingers near the nozzle.
LasersFire, and fumes from the wrong material. The beam itself stays inside a closed machine.Approved materials only. Lid closed. Stay with every job. Exhaust on.
CNC routerA bit spinning up to 24,000 RPM, flying chips, and noise.Safety glasses, hearing protection, hair tied back, hands out until the spindle stops. E-stop within reach.

If something goes wrong

What happensDo this
A print turns into a tangle of “spaghetti”Stop or pause it on the screen. Tell your teacher. Nobody gets hurt by a failed print.
A laser flame is bigger than a match flame, or won’t go outKeep the lid closed. Press Stop (Ador: the red E-stop). Tell your teacher right away. Your teacher handles the extinguisher.
The CNC bit grabs the part, or the sound changes to a scream or bangPress the red E-stop. Step back. Tell your teacher.
A burn, cut, or anything in your eyeTell your teacher right away, even if it seems small.

🧑‍🏫 For the teacher: setting up the license system

  • Post a printed sign-off sheet by each machine. A student’s license is live only after you sign it.
  • Keep the license quizzes in Canvas as 0-point, pass/fail assignments (one per machine), so you can see who’s licensed at a glance.
  • Keep a lab log (a clipboard or a Google Form) for problems: what happened, which machine, what was done.
  • Keep a CO₂ fire extinguisher near the lasers, and a first-aid kit in the room.
