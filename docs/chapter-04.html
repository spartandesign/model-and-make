Watch first: getting around Onshape

Read the transcript

This week, the computer holds your dimensions instead of the caliper. You'll work in Onshape, right in your browser. First, learn to move around your model. Right-drag to rotate and see every side. Middle-drag to pan, and slide the view. Scroll to zoom in close. A Part Studio is where you make parts. An Assembly is where you put parts together, using mates. Before you use the Measure tool, predict the distance between the two hole centers. Write it in your notebook. Then measure it on screen, in inches, to two decimals. When we print parts later, the caliper should read the same number. Watch for interference: two solids in the same space. On screen it looks fine, but real parts won't fit. Too tight is interference. Too loose is slop. You'll hear those words all semester.

Get set up first

  • Join the class. Open Onshape in your browser (no install) and join with the class code. Cloud autosave means you won’t lose work — but you need a live connection.
  • Set your units to inches. In your workspace, set units → inches. This course measures in inches (same as your calipers), so every on-screen measurement should read in inches.

Do the Onshape lessons (1.1–1.4)

📚 Onshape lessons

Onshape Intro to CAD — Unit 1: Jumping Into Onshape. Use lessons 1.1–1.4. Each lesson has a slide deck and a student guide.

Copy the workspace, follow along on the bike model, and submit each exit ticket.

  • 1.1 Jumping Into Onshape. Tour the interface; pan, zoom, rotate; do the comment + tag scavenger hunt.
  • 1.2 Why CAD? Views, the View Cube, Part Studio vs. Assembly, and the Measure tool.
  • 1.3 Creating 3D Models. Make 3D parts and assemble the pedals using mates.
  • 1.4 Modifying Your Models. Edit a feature, check for interference, and see how 2D drawings come from a model.

Two things you add (don’t skip)

  • Measurement bridge (in 1.2). Onshape has you measure the distance between two hole centers on the bike link. Predict it in your notebook first, then measure on screen and record it in inches to two decimals. The model holds the number now — when we print in Weeks 5 and 9, the caliper has to read the same number.
  • Interference (in 1.4). In your own words: what is interference, and what would it mean if you tried to manufacture the part for real? (Two solids in the same space is fine on screen, but the real parts won’t fit.) This is the tolerance idea you’ll use all course: too tight = interference, too loose = slop.

Looking ahead: the mates from 1.3 return when you build the skateboard and the capstone skate park, and the 2D-drawing idea from 1.4 comes back in Week 11.
